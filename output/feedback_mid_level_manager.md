# Feedback: Mid-Level Manager (Sarah Jenkins)

## Overall Impression

Bluefin is targeting a real organizational problem — the gap between what AI *could* do for teams and what it *actually* does today. As a manager of 12 engineers and analysts, I'm constantly looking for tools that help my team work smarter, not harder. The multi-agent orchestration concept is compelling, but I need to evaluate it through the lens of team adoption, integration with our existing stack, and measurable ROI.

## What I Liked

- **The core value proposition is clear and relevant.** My team spends hours on tasks that could be automated — synthesizing meeting notes, generating status reports, cleaning data for analysis. If Bluefin can orchestrate multi-step AI workflows that handle these tasks end-to-end, that's a genuine productivity gain for my entire team.
- **"Business-friendly outputs" is exactly what I need.** My team produces deliverables in specific formats — Confluence pages, Jira tickets, slide decks. The fact that Bluefin outputs in DOCX and Google Docs formats suggests they understand that the last mile of AI output matters. Markdown doesn't cut it in a corporate environment.
- **Cost-conscious model selection addresses a real concern.** I'm responsible for my team's tool budget. If agents automatically route to cost-efficient models, I don't have to worry about someone on my team accidentally running up a $500 GPT-4 bill on a task that GPT-3.5 could handle.
- **The presentation is professional and polished.** This matters more than people think. When I'm evaluating tools for my team, I need something I can show in a leadership meeting without embarrassment. Bluefin's marketing sets a high bar that signals the product might meet a similar standard.

## What Concerns Me

- **Team adoption is my biggest worry, and it's not addressed.** My team has varying comfort levels with AI tools. Some engineers are power users; some analysts are skeptical. Any new tool needs to have an intuitive UI, clear onboarding, and a gentle learning curve. The demo shows a beautiful marketing site, but it doesn't show me the actual product interface. How long does it take a new user to set up their first agent workflow? 5 minutes? 30 minutes? A day?
- **Integration with our existing tools is non-negotiable.** We live in Slack, Jira, Confluence, and Google Workspace. If Bluefin is a standalone app that requires my team to switch contexts, adoption will fail. I need to see Slack notifications, Jira integration, and Confluence output. The pitch mentions Google Docs — what about the rest of the Atlassian stack?
- **Security and compliance need to be explicit.** I manage a team that handles proprietary data. I need to know: Is it SOC 2 compliant? Can it be deployed within our corporate VPC? What's the data retention policy? My CISO will ask these questions before I can even pilot it.
- **How do I manage and monitor agent workflows across my team?** As a manager, I need visibility. Who's running what workflows? How much are we spending? Are the outputs meeting quality standards? I need a team dashboard, not just individual user accounts.
- **What happens when an agent makes a mistake?** My team's work feeds into executive presentations and client deliverables. If an agent hallucinates a data point and it gets into a report, that's my problem. I need rollback, audit trails, and quality checkpoints — not just autonomous execution.

## Questions for the Bluefin Team

1. What does the team management dashboard look like? Can I see usage, spending, and output quality across all team members?
2. How long does onboarding take for a non-technical team member?
3. What integrations are available today vs. on the roadmap? (Specifically: Slack, Jira, Confluence, Asana)
4. Can I set approval workflows — e.g., agent outputs need manager sign-off before being shared?
5. What's the pricing model? Per-seat? Per-agent-run? Enterprise tier?
6. Is there a pilot program for teams of 10-15 people?

## Suggestions

1. **Build a team management layer.** Usage dashboards, spending controls, approval workflows, and quality metrics. This is what makes a tool go from "nice for individuals" to "essential for teams."
2. **Prioritize Slack and Jira integration.** These are the two tools my team lives in. If Bluefin can receive prompts via Slack and create Jira tickets from agent output, adoption friction drops to near zero.
3. **Create team onboarding templates.** Pre-built workflows for common management tasks: "Weekly Team Status Report," "Sprint Retrospective Summary," "Stakeholder Feedback Synthesis." Let my team start with something useful immediately.
4. **Add approval checkpoints.** Let me configure workflows so that certain outputs require manager review before proceeding. This gives me control without bottlenecking the team.
5. **Publish a security whitepaper.** I need something I can hand to my CISO. SOC 2 certification, data handling policies, deployment options — make it easy for me to get internal approval.

## Verdict

**7/10** — Bluefin is solving the right problem for the right audience. The multi-agent orchestration concept, combined with business-friendly outputs and cost management, addresses the gap between individual AI tools and team-level productivity. But the missing pieces — team management, integrations, security documentation, and a clear onboarding path — are exactly the things that determine whether a tool gets adopted or abandoned in a corporate environment. I'd pilot this with a small team in a heartbeat if I could get answers on security and integration. Show me the admin dashboard and the Jira integration, and I'll champion it to leadership.

> "Will this help my team work faster or better?" — the potential is there, but I need to see the product, not just the pitch.
