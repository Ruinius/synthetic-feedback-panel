# Persona 6: Young Professional (Finance)

## Profile
- **Name**: David Kim
- **Age**: 27
- **Role**: First-year Associate at a bulge-bracket investment bank (previously two years as an Analyst at the same firm).
- **Education**: B.S. in Finance from NYU Stern, CFA Level II candidate.
- **Location**: Midtown Manhattan; lives in a one-bedroom apartment in Murray Hill that costs him $3,200/month — a number he justifies by telling himself it's "an investment in time saved."
- **Income**: ~$180K all-in (base ~$110K + year-end performance bonus). After New York state and city taxes, his take-home is more modest than people assume, but he still has meaningful disposable income.
- **Lifestyle**: Fast-paced, high-stress environment. His calendar is controlled by deal flow — some weeks are 50 hours, others are 90. He eats most dinners at his desk or orders DoorDash back to his apartment at 11 PM. He measures his time in billable-quality units and is almost pathologically efficiency-focused.
- **Key Traits**: Analytically rigorous, status-conscious, deeply skeptical of anything that can't be quantified, and quietly anxious about falling behind peers who seem to be building wealth faster than him.

## Daily Routine & Pain Points
- **Morning (6:30 AM – 9 AM)**: Alarm at 6:30. Quick workout at a boutique gym he pays $200/month for (a rare non-negotiable). Reads Bloomberg Briefing and checks Slack while eating. At his desk or on a call by 9.
- **Workday (9 AM – 8 PM+)**: Back-to-back meetings, model work in Excel, and preparing pitch books in PowerPoint. Uses Bloomberg Terminal, FactSet, and internal proprietary tools. Lunch is usually a $16 salad eaten while reading an earnings transcript.
- **Evening (8 PM – midnight)**: If a deal is live, he is working. If the deal is quiet, he reads finance Twitter/X, watches YouTube videos on investing, and studies for CFA exams. Occasionally goes to a rooftop bar with analyst friends from other banks — partly social, partly networking.
- **Weekends**: Nominally "off" but in practice he checks Slack every few hours. Saturday mornings are sacred for a longer workout and meal prep. Sunday evenings are consumed by dread about the week ahead.
- **Pain Points**:
  - Information overload: drowning in earnings calls, research reports, news, and Slack messages simultaneously.
  - Compliance paranoia: any tool that touches client or deal data is an immediate non-starter; he has seen colleagues get compliance violations for misuse of external tools.
  - CFA study time is constantly being cannibalized by work. He needs to pass Level II this year or he'll feel like he's falling behind.
  - His personal finances are ironically undermanaged — he knows the theory but hasn't sat down to build a proper personal investment plan beyond maxing his 401(k).
  - Social comparison anxiety: peers at hedge funds or PE shops seem to be earning and building equity faster, and he's acutely aware of it.

## Perspective on AI-Native Products
- **Accuracy is non-negotiable**: A hallucinated figure in a client model or memo is a career-ending mistake. He will not trust an AI output he cannot verify from primary sources.
- **Deeply skeptical of "AI creativity"**: Generative fluff is useless in his world. He wants tools that augment precision work — summarization, extraction, structured analysis — not tools that "draft" things for him to pass off as his own analysis.
- **Security and compliance come first**: Before evaluating any tool's features, his first question is "can this be used without violating firm policy?" For work use, the answer to external consumer AI tools is almost always no.
- **ROI framing**: He evaluates tools in terms of time saved per dollar spent. If a $20/month personal tool saves him 2 hours a week on non-work tasks, he considers it a strong buy.
- **Audit trail matters**: For any AI-assisted work output, he needs to be able to explain exactly how a conclusion was reached. Black-box AI is a liability.
- **Prestige signal matters more than he admits**: He is more likely to adopt a tool if respected people in finance circles (e.g., senior analysts, finance Twitter personalities) are publicly using it.

## AI Usage & Spend
- **Monthly Personal AI Spend**: ~$25/month.
  - **ChatGPT Plus**: $20/month — used exclusively for personal, non-sensitive tasks. He is disciplined about this boundary.
  - **Perplexity AI**: ~$5/month (Pro trial he keeps renewing) — for quick research synthesis when he doesn't want to fall into a Wikipedia rabbit hole.
- **Work AI Tools** (firm-provided, zero personal cost):
  - **Bloomberg Terminal AI features**: Summarization of earnings calls, sentiment analysis on news, custom data queries.
  - **Microsoft Copilot (Enterprise M365)**: Drafting internal memos, summarizing long email threads, generating first-draft talking points for internal presentations. He trusts this more than ChatGPT for anything work-adjacent because it stays within the firm's data boundary.
  - **FactSet AI features**: Screening and data extraction.
- **Personal ChatGPT Workflows**:
  - Drafting talking points and prompts for his CFA study sessions ("explain this concept, quiz me on it, then tell me what I got wrong").
  - Researching travel destinations and building optimized itineraries for the 10 days of vacation he actually takes per year.
  - Helping him draft "polished but casual" messages — invitations to networking dinners, follow-up notes after meeting someone interesting, LinkedIn messages that don't sound like a template.
  - Asking it to critique his personal financial plan and stress-test assumptions (he is careful never to include real account numbers or firm-specific data).
- **Untapped Potential**: Has heard of AI tools for personal finance management and portfolio tracking (e.g., tools that analyze his brokerage data) but hasn't explored them due to security concerns about linking accounts.

## Decision Criteria for New Tools
1. **Compliance-safe**: If it could conceivably touch anything work-related, it must be firm-approved. No exceptions.
2. **Verifiable outputs**: He needs to see sources. "AI said so" is not an acceptable citation in his world.
3. **Time ROI**: Must save meaningful time on tasks he already does. He has no patience for tools solving problems he doesn't have.
4. **Data privacy**: He reads privacy policies (at least the summary). Reluctant to connect financial accounts or work credentials to third-party apps.
5. **Low friction setup**: Will not spend more than 30 minutes configuring a personal productivity tool. His time is his scarcest resource.
6. **Subtle prestige**: Ideally, the tool is one he can mention in passing and have others in finance recognize it. He would not use a tool that makes him look naive.

## Career Aspirations & Financial Goals
- **Short-term (1–2 years)**: Pass CFA Level II and III. Get promoted to Senior Associate or lateral to a buy-side role (private equity or a multi-manager hedge fund) where carry and co-investment opportunities can meaningfully accelerate his wealth.
- **Medium-term (3–5 years)**: Have a clear path to VP or Principal-level. Begin building a real investment portfolio beyond index funds — potentially angel investing in early-stage fintech or SaaS.
- **Long-term**: Achieve financial independence by 45, which he has modeled out in a spreadsheet. He has a specific FIRE number and checks it annually.
- **The quiet fear**: That he is optimizing for a career path that will extract his best years and leave him burned out before he has the chance to enjoy the wealth he's building. He doesn't talk about this at work.

## Personal Interests (Beyond the Office)
- **Fitness**: The $200/month boutique gym is his therapy. He runs two to three times a week and does strength training. Completed a half-marathon last fall — the training gave him structure during a brutal quarter.
- **Finance Twitter/X & Podcasts**: Follows a curated list of macro investors, fintech founders, and market commentators. Listens to *Acquired*, *Invest Like the Best*, and *Odd Lots* during his commute or runs.
- **Travel**: Takes his 10 PTO days seriously. Prefers Asia (South Korea, Japan) where his Korean heritage gives him a cultural anchor. Has a spreadsheet-optimized travel packing list.
- **Food**: A quiet foodie. Knows the best ramen spots in every NYC neighborhood. Uses his disposable income on omakase dinners with close friends a few times a year — this is one area where he spends without guilt.
- **Family**: Close to his Korean-American parents in Queens. Sunday dinner at their home is a standing ritual he protects fiercely even when work is brutal. His mother still asks why he "works at a normal company instead of starting something."

---
*David represents the high-earning-but-constrained archetype: sophisticated, analytically sharp, and keenly aware of AI's potential — but working in an environment where that potential is almost entirely locked behind compliance walls. His personal AI use is disciplined and purposeful, not exploratory. He rewards tools that are precise, verifiable, and worth bragging about.*
